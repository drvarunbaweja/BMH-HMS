👁 What is Glaucoma?

Glaucoma (Kala Motia) is a group of eye diseases where raised intraocular pressure (IOP) silently damages the optic nerve — the cable that carries images from the eye to the brain.

Because peripheral vision is lost first, most patients notice nothing until 40–50% of nerve fibres are already destroyed. Lost vision cannot be restored — only future loss can be prevented.

  • Affects 1 in 40 adults over 40 worldwide
  • Normal IOP: 10–21 mmHg; treated target: ≤15–18 mmHg
  • Risk factors: family history, thin cornea, myopia, diabetes

Visual Field (Perimetry)

Maps the full field of vision. Glaucoma causes arcuate scotomas — crescent-shaped blind spots. Repeated tests track progression.

OCT RNFL

Measures retinal nerve fibre layer in microns. Detects glaucoma before visual field loss (up to 5 years earlier). Inferior thinning is the earliest sign.

Pachymetry

Measures corneal thickness. Thin corneas (<510 µm) give falsely low IOP readings — a hidden risk factor for glaucoma damage despite "normal" pressure.

⚙️ What is the Hydrus?

The Hydrus Microstent is an 8 mm nitinol (nickel-titanium) scaffold — thinner than a human hair — placed inside Schlemm's canal through the same tiny incision used for cataract surgery.

  • Spans 3 clock-hours of Schlemm's canal (widest of any microstent)
  • Largest inner lumen — maximises aqueous drainage
  • No external bleb · No implant outside the eye
  • Takes only 5–10 extra minutes at end of cataract surgery
  • MRI-safe · Biocompatible · Permanent
📅 Follow-up Schedule
  • 📍 Day 1 — IOP check, slit-lamp, drops review
  • 📍 Week 1 — IOP, corneal clarity, anterior chamber
  • 📍 Month 1 — IOP target check, taper drops
  • 📍 Month 3 — Repeat visual field + OCT RNFL
  • 📍 6-Monthly — Lifelong IOP monitoring
⚖️ Treatment Options — Side by Side
Treatment How it Works IOP Reduction Key Concern
Eye Drops Reduce aqueous production / increase outflow 20–30% Compliance, side-effects, lifelong cost
SLT Laser Opens trabecular meshwork 25–30% Effect wears off in 3–5 yrs; repeatable
Trabeculectomy Creates external drainage bleb 40–50% Bleb infection (blebitis), hypotony, frequent visits
iStent inject 2 small trabecular bypass stents 25–35% Smaller scaffold, less canal coverage
XEN Gel Stent Sub-conjunctival micro-tube 35–45% External bleb, needling often needed
Hydrus ✓ 8 mm canal scaffold — widest drainage 77% at 2 yrs Safest MIGS profile; no external bleb
🏥 Day of Surgery
  • 1 Fast for 4–6 hours (water/medication allowed)
  • 2 Arrive 1 hour early; dilating drops instilled
  • 3 Topical anaesthesia — no injection, no patch
  • 4 Cataract removed (phaco) → IOL + Hydrus inserted
  • 5 Total procedure: 30–45 minutes
  • 6 IOP checked; same-day discharge in most cases
✅ Post-op Instructions
  • Antibiotic + steroid drops — 4–6 weeks as prescribed
  • Do not rub the eye — use protective shield at night for 1 week
  • Dark glasses outdoors for 2 weeks
  • No swimming / dusty environments for 4 weeks
  • Avoid straining / heavy lifting for 1 week
  • Continue existing glaucoma drops until told to stop
  • Seek urgent review if: sudden pain, severe redness, vision loss
Important Note
Glaucoma is a lifelong condition. The Hydrus lowers IOP but does not restore lost vision. Drops may still be needed — but far fewer. Lifelong 6-monthly reviews are essential.
